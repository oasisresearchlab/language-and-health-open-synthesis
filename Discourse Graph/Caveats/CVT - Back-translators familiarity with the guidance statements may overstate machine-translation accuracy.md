## Limitation

All back-translators were employed at a pediatric hospital and were therefore likely already familiar with the anticipatory guidance safety statements being translated. That prior knowledge could have let them reconstruct the intended meaning from garbled machine output more readily than an actual limited-English-proficiency parent could, so the recorded accuracy scores may overstate how well the machine translations would communicate to the LEP caregivers the resources are meant for — biasing the study toward underestimating the real-world harm of machine translation.

## Supporting Quote

> "Furthermore, the participants in this study were all employed at a pediatric hospital and likely to be familiar with the anticipatory guidance statements. The knowledge of these statements could have allowed participants to more easily interpret the machine translations than LEP parents are able, thus producing back-translations that were more accurate and representative of the safety guidelines than the actual translations." (Das, 2019, p. 249)
