## Limitation

The cost comparison is described by the authors as minimal and preliminary rather than a complete cost-effectiveness analysis. It counts the eliminated vendor translation charge but does not fully model the offsetting and overhead costs of the machine-translation-plus-postediting workflow (e.g., postediting labor, quality assurance, tooling, and staff training), so the reported cost advantage of MT should be read as an indicative direction, not a validated net-savings estimate.

## Supporting Quote

> "The cost analysis is minimal and not intended to represent a complete cost-effectiveness analysis but rather a preliminary indication of cost reductions that can be achieved through MT with postediting." (Turner, 2014, p. 528)
