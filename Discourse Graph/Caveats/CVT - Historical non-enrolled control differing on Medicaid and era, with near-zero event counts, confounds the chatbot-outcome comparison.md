## Limitation

[Inferred:] The chatbot-versus-control outcome comparisons are confounded by the comparison design rather than by chatbot exposure alone. The control was a *historical* cohort operated on in 2018-2019, whereas the chatbot cohort was operated on in 2020-2022 — so the intervention is entangled with secular/era effects (including COVID-19-era changes in postoperative care and ED/readmission thresholds). The two groups were also not balanced: chatbot-enrolled patients were significantly more likely to have Medicaid (36.2% vs 16.2%, P = .0141). Finally, the favorable outcome rates rest on near-zero event counts (0/47 readmissions, 0/47 ED visits reported as 0.9%, 0/47 reoperations in the chatbot arm), making the rate estimates and P values fragile and imprecise. Together these features warrant treating the readmission benefit as association, not established causal effect.
