## Limitation

The study measured comprehension only after counseling and did not assess subjects' baseline knowledge of colposcopy or their change in knowledge. The authors note more educated patients may have researched colposcopy beforehand, giving them greater baseline knowledge; without a baseline measure it is impossible to know whether the observed education effect (and the post-counseling scores generally) reflects knowledge acquired during provider counseling or pre-existing knowledge. This directly undercuts causal interpretation of education as a predictor of consent comprehension.

## Supporting Quote

> "Also, we did not assess subjects' baseline knowledge about colposcopy or change in knowledge. More educated patients may have been more likely to research colposcopy before their procedure, resulting in an overall greater baseline knowledge. … Without measuring baseline knowledge, it is impossible to know whether the outcomes we measured are due to baseline knowledge before colposcopy or to knowledge obtained during counseling with a provider." (Krankl, 2011, p. 85)
