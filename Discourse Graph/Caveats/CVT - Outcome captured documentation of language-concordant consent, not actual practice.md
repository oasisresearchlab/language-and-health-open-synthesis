## Limitation

The study measured only *documentation* of language-concordant informed consent, not the actual consent practice. Documented rates can under-represent true language-concordant care (more concordant discussions may have occurred than were documented), and — conversely — the post-eConsent gain may partly reflect improved documentation of interpreter use rather than a real change in interpreter use. The documented-consent measure is therefore a proxy for, not a direct measure of, language-concordant care delivered.

## Supporting Quote

> "In addition, because only documentation of language-concordant informed consent was studied, it is possible that more language-concordant discussions took place that needed to be documented." (Trang, 2024, p. 531)

> "we speculate that many of the prior gaps were related to failing to document interpreter use on the paper consent as opposed to failure to use an interpreter in the consent discussion." (Trang, 2024, p. 530)
