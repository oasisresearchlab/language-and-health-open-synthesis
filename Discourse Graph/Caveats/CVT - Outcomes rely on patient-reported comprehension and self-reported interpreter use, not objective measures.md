## Limitation

The informed-consent outcomes are patient-reported comprehension — patient perception of whether they understood the reasons and risks and had their questions answered — rather than objective measures of knowledge, and the authors caution this perception may not correlate with objective knowledge (though they argue perception is itself a crucial, if imperfect, measure of whether consent was informed). Likewise, the study lacked objective measures of professional interpreter use during consent discussions and relied on patient self-report, so the interpreter-use estimates are vulnerable to recall and reporting error (compounded by missing interpreter-use data for a number of participants).

## Supporting Quote

> "Third, we did not have objective measures of professional interpreter use during informed consent discussions, and we relied on patient-reported comprehension, which represents patient perception and may not correlate with objective measures of knowledge. However, the patient's perception is a crucial, even if imperfect, measure of whether consent was actually informed." (Lee, 2017, p. 869)
