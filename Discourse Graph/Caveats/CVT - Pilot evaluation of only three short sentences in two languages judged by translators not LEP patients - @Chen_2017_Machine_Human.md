## Limitation

This was a pilot study whose evidence rests on only three short spoken sentences from a single diabetes patient-education pamphlet, translated into just two languages (Spanish and Chinese). Because the sentence sample is so small and language-limited, the authors caution the findings should not be generalized to other languages or to longer, real-world clinical communication. In addition, translation quality was rated by certified translators rather than measured as comprehension or outcomes in actual LEP patients, so the app's usefulness in real patient encounters remains untested.

## Supporting Quote

> "This pilot study has several limitations. First, this study assessed the quality of the iTranslate mobile language translation app using only three spoken sentences from a diabetes patient education pamphlet." (Chen, 2017, p. 8)

> "Second, we assessed only Spanish and Chinese translations so that the findings should not be applied to other languages." (Chen, 2017, p. 8)

> "Also, more studies should explore the app using experience from patients with LEP in various languages." (Chen, 2017, p. 8)
