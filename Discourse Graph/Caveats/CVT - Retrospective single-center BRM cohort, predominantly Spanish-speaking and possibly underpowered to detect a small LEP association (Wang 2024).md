## Limitation

The null LEP associations come from a retrospective, observational, single-institution cohort — not a randomized trial — so measured and unmeasured confounders could not be fully controlled despite multivariable adjustment. The authors explicitly note the sample may be inadequate to detect a small but genuine LEP effect, which is the central threat to interpreting these nulls as true absence of effect. The cohort was also predominantly Spanish-speaking, precluding stratification by language, and drawn from a single urban academic center with an LEP rate nearly double the U.S. average, limiting generalizability (especially to rural settings and non-Spanish LEP groups). Together these features make the null findings hypothesis-consistent but not confirmatory of "no effect."

## Supporting Quote

> "Limitations to this study include its retrospective and observational nature. Although multivariable regression modeling was employed in analysis to adjust for potential confounders, this study design was not a randomized control trial, and thus all measured and unmeasured covariates could not be adjusted for." (Fei, 2024, p. 694)

> "Lastly, there remain a possibility that our sample size is inadequate to detect a small but genuine association with LEP." (Fei, 2024, p. 694)

> "This study mainly focuses on Spanish speaking patients as we did not have sufficient sample size for other language groups, consequently, we were unable to stratify by language and attain further nuanced results." (Fei, 2024, p. 694)
