## Limitation

Translation A (the literal version) was a real published translation that contained spelling errors, which the authors retained because their interest was the translation approach rather than spelling. However, those spelling errors are a confound: they could independently have depressed participants' evaluations of translation A, so part of the observed preference and issue-count gap may reflect spelling rather than the literal-versus-functionalist approach. The authors recommend using an error-free text in future studies.

## Supporting Quote

> "Nonetheless, as indicated above, spelling could have influenced some participants' perceptions, and consequently, a text without spelling errors should be selected in future studies." (Colina, 2022, p. 36)
