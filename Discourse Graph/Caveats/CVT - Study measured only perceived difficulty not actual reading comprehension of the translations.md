## Limitation

The study captured only participants' perceptions of difficulty and their stated preferences; it included no reading-comprehension test or readability instrument, so the findings speak to perceived difficulty and preference, not to objectively measured comprehension. The authors caution that translation B might be only perceived as more formal or difficult and note that future studies should test actual difficulty.

## Supporting Quote

> "Our study considered only perceived difficulty since it asked participants about their input and perceptions of the translated text and there was no instrument or question designed to assess actual difficulty, such as reading comprehension questions." (Colina, 2022, p. 36)
