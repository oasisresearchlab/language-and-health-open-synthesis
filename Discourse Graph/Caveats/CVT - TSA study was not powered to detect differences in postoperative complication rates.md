## Limitation

The a priori power analysis sized the cohorts (35 minimum per group; 41 enrolled) to detect a length-of-stay difference, not a complication-rate difference. The authors state the study was not powered to detect differences in complication rates and that larger studies may report conflicting data. Consequently the non-significant 30- and 90-day complication results (P = .36 and P = .26) cannot be interpreted as evidence that interpreter need is unrelated to complications — the study was likely underpowered for this secondary outcome, where the interpreter cohort still showed numerically higher complication counts.

## Supporting Quote

> "our study was not powered to detect differences in complication rates, and future studies with larger study populations may report conflicting data." (Kunze, 2023, p. 89)
