## Limitation

Translation quality was rated by three college-educated, bilingual, nonclinician research assistants comparing sentences against the English source — not by the limited-English-proficiency patients who would actually read the material. Patients may have lower or more variable medical understanding and educational attainment than the evaluators, so the finding that machine and professional translations scored comparably on adequacy and meaning does not establish that patients would understand them equally well. Comparable expert-rated quality is an upstream proxy for, not a measurement of, patient comprehension.

## Supporting Quote

> "It also remains to be seen how easily GT translations will be understood by patients, who may have variable medical understanding and educational attainment as compared to our evaluators." (Khanna, 2011, p. 524)
