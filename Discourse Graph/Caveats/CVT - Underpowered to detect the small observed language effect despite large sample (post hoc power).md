## Limitation

Despite the large overall sample (58,918 patients), the study is by the authors' own post hoc assessment underpowered to detect the small, non-significant language effect it observed. The design targets clinically meaningful differences of ≥2%; a true difference smaller than that (or concentrated in the tiny LEP subgroup) could be overlooked. This is the central interpretive caveat on all of the null language findings: absence of a detected association is not evidence of no association. The authors caution the results should be interpreted cautiously and note that post hoc power calculations are not recommended for definitive conclusions.

## Supporting Quote

> "Finally, despite our large sample size, post hoc analysis reveals that the study is underpowered to detect the observed, nonsignificant effect size, which is clinically irrelevant. While post hoc power calculations are not recommended for definitive conclusions, they highlight potential power issues. Although the study is designed to detect larger, clinically meaningful differences of 2% or more, the current results should be interpreted cautiously, as the study might overlook a significant difference that exists." (Sabra, 2025, p. 996)
