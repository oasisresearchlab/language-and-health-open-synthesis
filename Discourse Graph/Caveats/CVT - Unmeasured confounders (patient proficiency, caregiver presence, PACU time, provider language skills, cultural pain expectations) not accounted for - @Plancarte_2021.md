## Limitation

The adjusted models could not account for several potentially important confounders because they were not documented: provider/nursing proficiency in non-English languages, the pediatric patient's own language proficiency, whether a caregiver was at the bedside after surgery, the time each patient spent in the PACU, and the timing of PACU pain medications relative to floor transfer. The authors also raise possible cultural differences in expectations of pain relief. Any of these could partially explain the observed disparity in time to first analgesia, so the adjusted hazard ratio may still be confounded.

## Supporting Quote

> "We also could not factor in provider and nursing proficiency in languages other than English, because it is not recorded; therefore, we were unable to add it into our analysis. Another limitation is that it is also possible that there are cultural differences in the expectation of pain relief that might have contributed to our findings. Finally, there may be unmeasured confounders that were not accounted for in our analysis. For example, we also were unable to assess the pediatric patient's language proficiency or whether the caregiver was at bedside after surgery as it was not documented." (Plancarte, 2021, p. 1203)

> "We were unable to measure time each patient spent in the PACU postoperatively or the relationship of timing of PACU pain medications to transfer to the floor, both of which may influence their likelihood of receiving analgesia on the general inpatient unit." (Plancarte, 2021, p. 1203)
