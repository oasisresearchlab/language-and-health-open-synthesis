## Limitation

Accuracy was measured indirectly, by scoring English back-translations rather than the machine translations themselves, and the back-translators — though native speakers — were mostly physicians or nurses not formally trained in translation, drawn from small samples that varied in size across languages. As a result, each language's score reflects the back-translators' abilities and errors as well as the quality of the machine translation, and the small, inconsistent samples limit precision and cross-language comparability.

## Supporting Quote

> "The sample size of the back-translators for each language was relatively small and not consistent across languages." (Das, 2019, p. 249)

> "Additionally, although native speakers were recruited as back-translators, most participants were physicians or nurses and were not formally trained in translation. It is possible that errors were made during back-translation; thus, back-translation scores for each language reflect not only the quality of the machine-generated translations but also the translators' abilities." (Das, 2019, p. 249)
