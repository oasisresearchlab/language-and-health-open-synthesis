Having a consent document translated into a patient's primary language available at the time of study opening increases that patient's likelihood of enrolling in the trial. The effect is language-specific: it raises enrolment of speakers of the language actually translated, not of non-English-speaking patients in general.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Spanish-speaking patients had higher odds (OR 5.7) of signing consent for studies with Spanish consent available at study opening - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]

## Contradicting Evidence

- [[EVD - Patients with a primary language other than English or Spanish did not have higher odds (OR 0.9) of signing consent for studies with Spanish consent at opening - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
