Patient–provider language discordance (limited English proficiency) is associated with fewer postoperative opioid prescription refill requests after total knee arthroplasty, despite similar preoperative/postoperative pain levels and prior opioid use — suggesting a disparity in access to postoperative pain-medication rather than in clinical need.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - LEP patients requested fewer opioid refills than EP within 30 days (35.3% vs 52.4%) after knee arthroplasty - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
- [[EVD - LEP patients requested fewer opioid refills than EP within 60 days (48.7% vs 61.0%) after knee arthroplasty - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
- [[EVD - LEP patients requested fewer opioid refills than EP within 90 days (54.0% vs 62.9%) after knee arthroplasty - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
- [[EVD - LEP independently predicted lower odds of a 30-day opioid refill request (aOR 0.61) after knee arthroplasty - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
- [[EVD - Preoperative and postoperative pain levels did not differ between LEP and EP knee arthroplasty patients - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
- [[EVD - Prior opioid use did not differ between LEP and EP knee arthroplasty patients - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]

## Contradicting Evidence

- [[EVD - LEP was not independently associated with 60- or 90-day opioid refill requests after knee arthroplasty - @Nguyen_2023_Language_Barriers]]
