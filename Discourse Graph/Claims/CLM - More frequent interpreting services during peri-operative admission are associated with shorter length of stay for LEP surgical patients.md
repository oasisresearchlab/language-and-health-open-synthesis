Greater frequency of professional interpreting services (interpreting events per day) during a peri-operative admission is associated with shorter length of stay for surgical patients with limited English proficiency, in a graded (exposure-response) fashion, with the benefit concentrated at higher interpreting frequencies rather than at any use above baseline.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Unadjusted median peri-operative LOS fell monotonically from 11 to 1 day across ascending interpreting-frequency quartiles - @Claire_2022_Increasing_Frequency]]
- [[EVD - Highest interpreting-frequency quartile had 4.6-day shorter adjusted peri-operative LOS vs lowest - @Claire_2022_Increasing_Frequency]]
- [[EVD - Third interpreting-frequency quartile had 4.2-day shorter adjusted peri-operative LOS vs lowest - @Claire_2022_Increasing_Frequency]]
- [[EVD - Shorter LOS in top two interpreting-frequency quartiles was robust across five operative-complexity adjustment models - @Claire_2022_Increasing_Frequency]]

## Contradicting Evidence

- [[EVD - Second interpreting-frequency quartile showed no significant adjusted LOS difference vs lowest (P=0.37) - @Claire_2022_Increasing_Frequency]]
