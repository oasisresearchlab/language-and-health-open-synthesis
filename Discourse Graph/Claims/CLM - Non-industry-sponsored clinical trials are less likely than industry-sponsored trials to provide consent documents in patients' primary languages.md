Clinical trials not sponsored by industry are substantially less likely than industry-sponsored trials to have consent documents available in, and to obtain consent in, patients' primary languages — so patients with a primary language other than English who do enrol are more often asked to sign a consent document they cannot fully read.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Industry-sponsored studies had higher translated-consent availability (51.4% vs 23.9% of events; OR 3.4) than non-industry studies - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
- [[EVD - Primary language other than English independently predicted lower odds of signing consent in primary language (OR 0.38) for non-industry studies - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
- [[EVD - Limited English proficiency independently predicted lower odds of signing consent in primary language (OR 0.35) for non-industry studies - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
- [[EVD - Primary-language-other-than-English patients signed consent in a non-primary language in 72.6% of non-industry vs 43.8% of industry events - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
- [[EVD - LEP patients signed consent in a non-primary language in 65.9% of non-industry vs 31.9% of industry events - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
- [[EVD - Among 18 patients consenting to both sponsor types in discordant languages 16 signed non-primary for the non-industry study (McNemar P=0.002) - @Maria_2023_Consent_document]]
