Patients who need language assistance services (limited English proficiency) receive less postoperative opioid than English-proficient surgical patients. Evidence is mixed on where this holds: it may be observable for immediate PACU opioid administration while absent for total perioperative opioid consumption.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Patients needing language assistance had lower PACU opioid consumption (median 5 vs 10 MEDD, p=0.021) after matching - @Kapoor_2023_Impact_Need]]

## Contradicting Evidence

- [[EVD - Need for language assistance was not associated with total perioperative opioid use (33 vs 30 MEDD, p=0.099) after matching - @Kapoor_2023_Impact_Need]]
