Patients with limited English proficiency (LEP) can achieve equal or better clinical outcomes than English-proficient patients when they receive care at facilities that provide robust support services (including interpreter and social services), even though LEP populations carry greater socioeconomic risk.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Adults with LEP and diagnosed HIV were more virally suppressed than EP adults (77.5% vs 69.6%) - @Padilla_2021_Limited_English]]
- [[EVD - Adults with LEP were more likely to be prescribed ART than EP adults (89.7% vs 83.5%) but ART adherence did not differ - @Padilla_2021_Limited_English]]
- [[EVD - Adults with LEP were more likely than EP adults to receive care at RWHAP-funded facilities (79.0% vs 67.0%) - @Padilla_2021_Limited_English]]
