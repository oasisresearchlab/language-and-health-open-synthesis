Surgeons frequently obtain preoperative informed consent from LEP patients using ad-hoc interpreters (bilingual staff, adult family members, or minors) or their own limited non-English language skills rather than professional interpreters, and lean further on these ad-hoc alternatives when a professional interpreter is not available in a timely manner.

## Supporting Evidence

- [[EVD - Surgeons not fluent in non-English languages often used their limited skills to obtain preoperative informed consent from LEP patients - @Patel_2016_Preoperative_consent]]
- [[EVD - Many surgeons reported relying on bilingual staff and adult family members as ad-hoc interpreters for preoperative consent - @Patel_2016_Preoperative_consent]]
- [[EVD - Surgeons reported they would use minors as ad-hoc interpreters to obtain surgical consent, contravening hospital policy - @Patel_2016_Preoperative_consent]]
- [[EVD - When a professional interpreter was not available in a timely manner surgeons more frequently used ad-hoc interpreters or their own nonfluent skills - @Patel_2016_Preoperative_consent]]
