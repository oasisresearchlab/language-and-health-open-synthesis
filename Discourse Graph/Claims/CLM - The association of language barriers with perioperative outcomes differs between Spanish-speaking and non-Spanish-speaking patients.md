## Narrative synthesis

Five included studies subcategorized LEP patients into Spanish-speaking vs other languages, and the review repeatedly found that the direction and significance of associations depended on this subgroup: reduced access to care was significant only for non–Spanish-speaking LEP in one study; higher postoperative complications after neuro-oncologic surgery were specific to non–Spanish-speaking LEP; and, conversely, Spanish-speaking LEP was associated with lower odds of skilled-facility discharge and shorter LOS in some cohorts. This heterogeneity by language group is one reason the review declines to pool estimates. Certainty/GRADE appraisal is left for expert review.

> "Some additional differences in associations were found between patients with limited English proficiency who spoke Spanish vs other languages." (Joo, 2023, p. 1)

> "Five studies subcategorized the preferred language of patients with LEP into Spanish vs others. Studies that solely used ethnicity or country of birth as a proxy for language proficiency were excluded from this systematic review." (Joo, 2023, p. 6)
