## Description

When asked which of two Spanish translations of the Perceived Stress Scale they preferred overall, 15 of 20 bilingual participants (75%) chose the version produced with a functionalist (reader-oriented) approach ("translation B") over the version produced with a literal, word-for-word approach ("translation A"). The two translations were of the same source survey, so the preference reflects the translation approach rather than the underlying instrument.

> "Seventy-five percent (n = 15) of the participants preferred the translation that used a functionalist approach (B) over the word-for-word one (A)." (Colina, 2022, p. 33)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: each participant's stated overall preference between the two translations, elicited at the end of the interview.

> "At the conclusion of the interview, participants were asked to indicate which survey they preferred overall and why (see appendix B, "Sample Questions for Semistructured Interviews")." (Colina, 2022, p. 33)

### How?

Participants reviewed two translations of the same survey — a published literal translation ("A") and a newly drafted functionalist translation ("B") — then answered semistructured interview questions and stated a preference.

> "we used a published translation of the Perceived Stress Scale (see appendix A, available online, along with appendix B and table 1; see the "Supporting Information" section below), a patient-reported outcome measure that reflects a literal translation approach ("translation A," in appendix A) and drafted a new one ("translation B" in appendix A) using the functionalist approach described by Colina et al." (Colina, 2022, p. 32)

### Who?

Twenty English-Spanish bilingual adults (ages 19-39; 11 Spanish-dominant, 1 balanced, 8 English-dominant) recruited at and around the University of Arizona in Tucson.

> "We recruited 20 bilingual participants at the campus of the University of Arizona and in its surrounding community using flyers written in English and Spanish and by word of mouth." (Colina, 2022, p. 32)

## Other Notes

Both translations were from English into Mexican Spanish. Translation A was a published, real-world translation used in the field; translation B was drafted for the study using the functionalist approach.
