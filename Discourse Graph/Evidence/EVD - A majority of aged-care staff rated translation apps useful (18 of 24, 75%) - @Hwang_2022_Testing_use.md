## Description


![[Hwang_2022_Testing_use-table1.png]]
On the end-of-trial staff survey, a majority of respondents rated the translation apps useful: 18 of 24 (75%) rated them very, moderately, or slightly useful, while half (12/24, 50%) found the apps moderately easy to use (Table 1). This indicates broad staff-perceived acceptability of the apps for everyday ward communication.

> "A majority of staff found the translation apps useful (18/24, 75%)." (Hwang, 2022, p. 581)

> "with most finding the translation apps moderately easy to use (12/24, 50%)." (Hwang, 2022, p. 581)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: staff-rated usefulness and ease of use of the translation apps, recorded on ordinal survey scales (very / moderately / slightly useful / not useful; very easy → very difficult).

> "measuring their experience of using the translation app" (Hwang, 2022, p. 580)

### How?

A self-report staff survey administered at the end of the two-month app trial, summarized with descriptive statistics in SPSS.

> "Categorical and numerical data from the surveys and observations were collated using descriptive statistics using SPSS v25 (IBM Corp)." (Hwang, 2022, p. 580)

### Who?

The 24 nursing and allied health staff across four aged-care hospital wards at three Melbourne hospital sites who completed the survey; usefulness had 1 missing and ease-of-use 2 missing responses.

> "Twenty-four nursing and allied health staff across the four wards completed the staff survey." (Hwang, 2022, p. 581)

## Other Notes

Usefulness is a staff-perception measure, not a patient-outcome measure; the study did not directly capture patients' perceptions.
