## Description

![[Jaramillo_2016_Hispanic_Clinic-table3.png]]

On pairwise t-tests, both concordant groups asked significantly more questions than the LEP discordant-interpreter group: concordant-English asked 3.21 more questions than discordant (p = 0.002) and concordant-Spanish asked 2.56 more (p = 0.001) (Table 3). Interpreter-mediated (discordant) visits were associated with the fewest patient-initiated questions.

> "while both groups respectively asked significantly more questions compared to LEP Spanish-discordant participants (p = 0.002, p = 0.001)." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the count of patient-initiated questions per visit, contrasted between each concordant group and the discordant-interpreter group.

> "four trained research assistants listened to the audio recording of the encounter and measured the number of patient-asked questions related to eliciting medical information and the length of the visit with the surgeon." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

### How?

Pairwise Student's t-tests on audio-derived question counts. The discordant arm received the same surgical care mediated by a trained interpreter; the concordant-Spanish arm received care via [[ART - The Hispanic Clinic for Pediatric Surgery]].

> "We examined comparisons of continuous variables among the three exposure groups using one-way analysis of variance and pairwise analysis using student's t-tests." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

### Who?

The 156-family cohort at a single academic children's hospital: concordant-English (57), LEP concordant-Spanish (52), and LEP discordant-interpreter (47).

> "A total of 156 families were enrolled including 57 concordant-English, 52 LEP concordant-Spanish and 47 LEP discordant families." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

## Other Notes

The pairwise differences in average values (LC-Eng vs LD = 3.21; LC-Sp vs LD = 2.56) and their p-values are reported in Table 3.
