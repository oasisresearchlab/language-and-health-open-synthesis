## Description

![[Krankl_2011_Patient_predictors-table2.png]]

Across all 149 colposcopy patients (English and Spanish speakers combined), subjects correctly answered an average of 7.91 ± 2.16 of the 11 colposcopy knowledge-survey questions — 72% — leading the authors to conclude that many patients did not understand the procedure well enough to give informed consent. Per-group summary scores are tabulated (Table 2); the 72% overall figure is a text/abstract summary of the pooled sample.

> "Subjects correctly answered an average of 7.91 ± 2.16 (72%) of 11 colposcopy survey questions." (Krankl, 2011, p. 80)

> "On average, subjects correctly answered only 72% of the questions, confirming prior research that suggests that many women undergoing colposcopy do not fully understand the procedure." (Krankl, 2011, p. 83)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: a summary colposcopy knowledge score (0–11), one point per correctly answered survey question, quantifying comprehension of colposcopy for informed consent.

> "A colposcopy knowledge score was created for all subjects. Subjects were given 1 point for each correctly answered question, with the highest possible score being 11." (Krankl, 2011, p. 82)

### How?

A self-administered post-procedure survey of purpose, risks, benefits, alternatives, and nature of colposcopy, completed after the usual pre-colposcopy consent counseling, in a cross-sectional design.

> "After the colposcopy, subjects completed a brief survey (Figure 1) to assess their understanding of the purpose, risks, benefits, alternatives, and nature of colposcopy." (Krankl, 2011, p. 81)

### Who?

149 English- and Spanish-speaking colposcopy patients (≥18 years, fluent/literate in English or Spanish) with complete data, recruited at one tertiary care and one community hospital in Boston, June–August 2007.

> "We obtained complete data on 111 English speakers and 38 Spanish speakers." (Krankl, 2011, p. 80)
