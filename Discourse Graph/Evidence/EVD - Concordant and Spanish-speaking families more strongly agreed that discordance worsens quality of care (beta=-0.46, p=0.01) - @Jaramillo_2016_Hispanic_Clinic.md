## Description

![[Jaramillo_2016_Hispanic_Clinic-table5.png]]

In the OLS survey model, agreement that quality of care is worsened by language discordance ("QOC worsened by lang discordance") was significantly predicted by both language concordance (β = −0.46, p = 0.01) and the patient speaking Spanish (β = 1.04, p < 0.001) (Table 5). Both concordant and Spanish-speaking families more strongly endorsed the view that discordant care degrades quality.

> "QOC worsened by lang discordance     −0.46     0.01     1.04     < 0.001" (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 674, Table 5)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the participant's 5-point Likert rating on the "quality of care worsened by language discordance" survey item.

> "Participants indicated their level of agreement with a series of 15 questions using a 5-point Likert scale from 1 (strongly disagree/very negative/never) to 5 (strongly agree/very positive/always)." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

### How?

A surgery-specific post-visit satisfaction survey analyzed by OLS regression using language concordance and patient's language as explanatory variables. The concordant-Spanish arm received care via [[ART - The Hispanic Clinic for Pediatric Surgery]].

> "Regression modeling was used to determine the factors associated with survey results using language concordance and patient's language as explanatory variables." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

### Who?

The 156-family cohort of pediatric surgery patients at a single academic children's hospital, surveyed after their visit.

> "A total of 156 families were enrolled including 57 concordant-English, 52 LEP concordant-Spanish and 47 LEP discordant families." (Jaramillo, 2016, p. 672)

## Other Notes

Values are read directly from Table 5, row "QOC worsened by lang discordance": language concordance −0.46 (p = 0.01), patient's language 1.04 (p < 0.001). This survey item has no dedicated sentence in the Results text.
