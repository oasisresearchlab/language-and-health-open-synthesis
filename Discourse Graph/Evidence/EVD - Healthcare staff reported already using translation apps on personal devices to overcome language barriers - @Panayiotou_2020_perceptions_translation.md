## Description

Some healthcare staff reported that they already used mobile translation apps with LEP patients in the workplace, on their own personal devices, to overcome communication barriers when an interpreter was not available. The most commonly used apps were Google Translate, TalkToMe and iTranslate.

> "To overcome these barriers, some healthcare staff reported using translation apps with patients in the healthcare setting. The most commonly used apps were Google Translate™, TalkToMe and iTranslate, which healthcare workers reported using on their own personal devices." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3523)

> "One participant described a positive experience of using the real-time voice-to-voice translation feature of Google Translate™ to communicate basic needs with a Spanish speaking patient who was unable to read" (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3521)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: healthcare workers' self-reported current use of mobile translation apps in the workplace to communicate with LEP patients.

> "Have you ever used or experienced a healthcare worker using technology to translate information? Please provide some information about how this experience was for you." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3518)

### How?

Focus group consultations at three hospital sites eliciting current practices and experiences with translation technology, analysed by inductive content analysis. Apps referenced include [[ART - Google Translate]], [[ART - Talk To Me translation app]], and [[ART - iTranslate voice-enabled mobile language translation app]].

> "The nursing and allied health staff consultations were conducted at three hospital sites to improve the likelihood of nursing and allied health staff attending from each site." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3518)

### Who?

Seventeen nursing and allied health staff (82.4% nurses) from three Melbourne metropolitan public health services, over half with 11+ years in health care.

> "Seventeen healthcare staff from three Melbourne metropolitan public health services took part in the consultations." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3519)

## Other Notes

Qualitative current-practice finding; no associated figure/table (text-only). Reflects the paper's "some staff already report using translation apps" contribution statement.
