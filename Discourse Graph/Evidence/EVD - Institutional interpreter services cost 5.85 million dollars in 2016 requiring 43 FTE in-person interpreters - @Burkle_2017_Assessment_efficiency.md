## Description

Total institutional language-department costs for providing interpretive services across the medical campus in 2016 were calculated at $5,847,000, comprising $4,290,000 for in-person interpreter salary and benefits and $1,528,000 for VRI and telephone vendor services. Maintaining in-person interpreter availability for all patients required 43 full-time-equivalent (FTE) interpreters. Only a fraction of this campus-wide cost was directly attributable to the surgical/procedural areas surveyed. Reported in the Results and Discussion text; no dedicated figure or table.

> "Total institutional language departmental costs for providing interpretive services in the year 2016 were calculated to be $5,847,000. This included both $4,290,000 for salary and benefits of in-person interpreters and $1,528,000 for VRI and telephone vendor services." (Burkle, 2017, p. 3)

> "the availability of in person interpreters to meet the needs of all our patients requires a total of 43 full time employee equivalent interpreters." (Burkle, 2017, p. 4)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the annual institutional cost of providing interpretive services and the interpreter staffing (FTE) required.

> "we also explored the cost to our institution associated with providing interpretive services over the study period." (Burkle, 2017, p. 2)

### How?

Costs for 2016 were determined through analysis of departmental accounting datasheets (a descriptive cost accounting, not a formal economic evaluation).

> "Costs for providing interpretive services for 2016, the year in which the study took place, were determined through analysis departmental accounting datasheets." (Burkle, 2017, p. 2)

### Who?

The entire language-services department of a single private, not-for-profit, quaternary-care US medical campus (campus-wide, not restricted to the surgical/procedural cohort studied).

> "The total cost for providing interpretive services in 2016 at our medical campus was $5,847,000." (Burkle, 2017, p. 4)

## Other Notes

The authors note limited reimbursement: Medicare does not reimburse for language-access services, and the institution does not charge patients or insurers, though it may seek reimbursement from the State of Minnesota for patients on medical assistance.
