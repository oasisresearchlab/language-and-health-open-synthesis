## Description


![[Soller_2012_Performance_new-table8.png]]
The 21 LEP diabetes patients who completed the accuracy assessment reported high satisfaction with the S-MINDS device on an anonymous Spanish-language 5-point Likert survey, with mean scores of 4.7–4.9 across four domains (Table 8). The Spanish audio being easy to understand scored highest (mean 4.9, SD 0.5); the device being easy to use, giving a better understanding of the pharmacist's recommendations, and preferring the device each scored 4.7. All but two patients "totally agreed" that the Spanish instructions from the device were easy to understand.

> "Patient satisfaction was high (means of 4.7–4.9 over four domains on a 5-point Likert scale)." (Soller, 2012, p. 927)

> "Patient satisfaction mean scores were ≥4.7 on a 5-point Likert scale (Table 8). All except two patients "totally agreed" that instructions in Spanish from the device was easy to understand." (Soller, 2012, p. 933)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: patient-reported satisfaction with the device, captured as agreement on a five-point Likert scale across a series of statements.

> "Patients enrolled in the accuracy assessment (discussed earlier) were asked to complete an anonymous satisfaction survey in Spanish and formatted as a five-point Likert scale (i.e., totally agree, somewhat agree, uncertain, somewhat disagree, or totally disagree with a series of statements [discussed later])." (Soller, 2012, p. 929)

### How?

An anonymous self-administered Spanish-language survey given to the same patients who completed the accuracy assessment of the [[ART - S-MINDS concept-based speech translation system]], analyzed with descriptive statistics (means, SDs).

> "Statistical analysis involved standard descriptive methods (mean, standard deviation) and derivation of 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of proportions." (Soller, 2012, p. 929)

### Who?

The same 21 Spanish-preferring LEP type 2 diabetes patients (mainly women, virtually no English proficiency) from the UCSF–St. Anthony Diabetes Telepharmacy Clinic accuracy assessment.

> "Twenty-one patients met inclusion criteria. They were mainly women (60%) on multiple medications with virtually no English proficiency in speech or written language (Table 4)." (Soller, 2012, p. 930)

## Other Notes

Satisfaction was measured only in patients who had just completed a favorable accuracy session at a single clinic, with a $20 incentive; no comparator group (e.g., human interpreter) was surveyed.
