## Description


![[Narang_2019_Use_Mobile-fig2.png]]
The origin of OPI calls shifted sharply toward the app once it was available (Fig. 2). During the intervention, 106 of 163 calls (65%) to the OPI service were placed via the mobile application, versus 48 (29%) by Vocera Badge and 9 (6%) by landline; app use rose further to 76% of calls postintervention. Before the app existed, calls were placed only by Vocera Badge (75%) or landline (25%), so the app became the predominant access modality and displaced the landline channel.

> "In the intervention period, 106 (65%) calls to the OPI service were placed using the mobile application, 48 (29%) via Vocera Badges, and 9 (6%) using landlines." (Narang, 2019, p. S187)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the origin (modality) of each OPI call — mobile application, landline phone, or Vocera Badge — and its share of calls in each study phase.

> "Origin of phone calls to the OPI service varied by the study phase (Fig. 2)." (Narang, 2019, p. S187)

### How?

The hospital interpreter-services program logged the total number of OPI calls and their channel, letting the investigators partition calls by whether they were placed through the app, a landline, or a Vocera Badge across the three phases.

> "We were able to distinguish whether calls to the OPI were made through the application, landline phone, or Vocera Badge." (Narang, 2019, p. S186)

### Who?

The 65 UCC clinicians at a New York City cancer center's Urgent Care Center, whose OPI calls were tracked across preintervention, intervention, and postintervention phases.

> "In total, 65 physicians, physician assistants, and nurses participated in the study out of 89 UCC clinicians." (Narang, 2019, p. S186)

## Other Notes

The modality shift complements the total-frequency finding: the app not only raised the volume of interpreter calls but became the channel through which most were placed.
