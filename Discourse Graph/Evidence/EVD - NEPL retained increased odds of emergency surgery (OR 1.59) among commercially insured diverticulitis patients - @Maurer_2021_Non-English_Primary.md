## Description

In a sensitivity analysis restricted to commercially insured patients only, NEPL retained a significantly higher odds of emergency versus elective surgery for diverticulitis compared to EPL (OR 1.59; 95% CI 1.23–2.04; P < 0.001). Within this insurance-homogeneous subset, non-Spanish NEPL patients again had significantly increased odds (OR 1.84; 95% CI 1.33–2.55; P < 0.001), while Spanish NEPL patients showed no significant difference (OR 1.27; 95% CI 0.86–1.88). The authors read this as evidence that insurance status is not the primary driver of the language association.

> "On multivariable analysis among patients with commercial insurance only, patients with NEPL had a significantly higher odds of emergency surgery versus patients with EPL (OR 1.59; 95% CI 1.23-2.04; P <0.001). Non-Spanish NEPL patients also continued to have a significantly increased odds of emergency surgery (OR 1.84; 95% CI 1.33-2.55; P <0.001), with no significant difference for Spanish NEPL patients (OR 1.27; 95% CI 0.86-1.88)." (Maurer, 2021, p. 645)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: emergency (urgent/emergent) versus elective surgical admission type, estimated within the commercially insured stratum only.

> "A sensitivity analysis was also performed only among patients with commercial insurance to determine the odds of emergency surgery in NEPL versus EPL patients, in addition to Spanish NEPL and non-Spanish NEPL versus EPL patients." (Maurer, 2021, p. 645)

### How?

Multivariable logistic regression identical in structure to the primary model but restricted to patients with commercial insurance, isolating the language effect from insurance-type confounding.

> "A sensitivity analysis was also performed only among patients with commercial insurance to determine the odds of emergency surgery in NEPL versus EPL patients." (Maurer, 2021, p. 645)

### Who?

The commercially insured subset of the 9,453-patient New Jersey diverticulitis surgery cohort; overall 5,507 (58.3%) of patients had commercial insurance, of whom 265 were NEPL.

> "Patients with NEPL were less likely to have commercial insurance (44.8% NEPL versus 59.2% EPL; P <0.001)" (Maurer, 2021, p. 645)

## Other Notes

Robustness/mechanism finding: because commercial insurance is held constant, the persisting NEPL association argues against insurance access as the sole explanation and supports language itself as a contributor. The Spanish NEPL point estimate rises to 1.27 here (from 0.80 overall) but remains non-significant.
