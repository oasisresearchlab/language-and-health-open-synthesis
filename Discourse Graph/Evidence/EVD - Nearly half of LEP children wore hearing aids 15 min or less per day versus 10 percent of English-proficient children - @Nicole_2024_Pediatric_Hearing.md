## Description

Beyond the mean difference, near-zero device use was far more common in the LEP group. Nearly half of the LEP children — 10 of 24 (42%) — wore their hearing aids for 15 minutes or less per day, compared with only about 10% (7 of 69) of children from English-proficient families. No child in the LEP group reached the ~10 hr/day benchmark associated with better language outcomes.

> "Moreover, nearly half of children in the limited-English proficiency group (n = 10 of 24; 42%) wore their hearing aids for 15 min or less each day as compared to only about 10% of children from English-proficient families (n = 7 of 69)." (Brigham, 2024, p. 325)

> "None of the children in the limited-English proficiency group demonstrated daily wear times of 10 or more hours a day." (Brigham, 2024, p. 325)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the proportion of children in each group with effectively negligible daily hearing aid use (data-logged wear time of 15 minutes or less per day), and the proportion reaching the 10 hr/day benchmark.

> "Moreover, nearly half of children in the limited-English proficiency group (n = 10 of 24; 42%) wore their hearing aids for 15 min or less each day as compared to only about 10% of children from English-proficient families (n = 7 of 69)." (Brigham, 2024, p. 325)

### How?

Retrospective chart review at one academic medical center; LEP status operationalized by interpreter presence at appointments, with daily wear time obtained from hearing aid data logging and children counted as near-nonusers if logged use was 15 minutes or less per day.

> "A retrospective chart review was completed to evaluate hearing aid wear time based on data logging information available at the time of a follow-up visit following an initial hearing aid fitting." (Brigham, 2024, p. 321)

### Who?

Children aged 2-60 months with permanent bilateral hearing loss seen for hearing aid follow-up between April 2020 and April 2022 at Vanderbilt University Medical Center (Nashville, TN): 69 from English-proficient families and 24 from LEP families (79% Spanish-speaking).

> "This chart review included children from one of two types of families: (a) families who were English proficient (n = 69) and (b) families with limited English proficiency (n = 24); with 79% of this group being Spanish speaking." (Brigham, 2024, p. 323)

## Other Notes

This distributional finding (mass at near-zero use) shows the group gap is not driven only by a few outliers but reflects widespread near-nonuse among LEP children. The authors interpret this as scarce access to sound throughout the day.
