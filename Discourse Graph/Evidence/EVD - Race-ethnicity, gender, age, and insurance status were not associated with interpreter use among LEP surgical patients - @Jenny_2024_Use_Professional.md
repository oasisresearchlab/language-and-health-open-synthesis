## Description

![[Jenny_2024_Use_Professional-table2.png]]

On univariate analysis, self-identified race and ethnicity, gender, age, and insurance status were **not** associated with interpreter use among the 117 LEP surgical patients (Table 2). This is a null result: apart from preferred language (Spanish/Chinese vs other), none of the a priori demographic or insurance covariates predicted whether an interpreter was documented. The language-group difference was also observed only for first-24-hour use and not for the other interpreter-use outcomes.

> "Race and ethnicity, gender, age, and insurance status were not associated with interpreter use in univariate analyses (Table 2)." (Cevallos, 2024, p. 2)

> "but these differences were not observed for any other of the assessed primary outcomes." (Cevallos, 2024, p. 2)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the association between a priori patient covariates (race/ethnicity, gender, age, insurance status) and documented interpreter use.

> "Primary variables chosen a priori according to prior literature were self-identified race and ethnicity and language, gender, age, and insurance status." (Cevallos, 2024, p. 1)

### How?

Univariate (unadjusted) analysis in Stata, testing each a priori covariate against interpreter-use outcomes; significance set at 2-sided P < .05.

> "Data were analyzed from May to October 2021 using univariate analysis with Stata statistical software version 16.1 (StataCorp). Significance was set at 2-sided P < .05." (Cevallos, 2024, p. 1)

### Who?

117 LEP patients aged ≥18 admitted for appendectomy, cholecystectomy, or colectomy at a single urban academic center, 2019–2020 (63.3% female; 53.9% White, 30.8% Latinx, 11.1% Asian; 53.0% Medicare, 28.2% Medicaid).

> "Of the 130 patients with LEP, the analytical cohort included 117 patients (74 female [63.3%]; mean [SD] age, 64 [17.3] years)." (Cevallos, 2024, p. 1)

## Other Notes

This null must **oppose** any claim that demographic/insurance characteristics predict interpreter access; it **supports** the claim that they do not.
