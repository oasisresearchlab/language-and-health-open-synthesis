## Description

In the thematic analysis of the interviews, participants characterized the functionalist translation B as easier to read, more reader-friendly, and sounding as though it had been written by a native Spanish speaker, while they described the literal translation A as awkward, overly direct, and structured like the English source. Some experienced the literal directness as offensive: one participant said form A felt "like an attack" and would read as a rejection to a Spanish speaker.

> "Participants referred to translation B overall as easier to read and more reader friendly, commenting, for example, "Easy to understand" (Participant 1), "User friendly, easy to follow" (Participant 2), "Better flow, the ideas came clear [sic] and understandable" (Participant 10), and, "[L]o entendí mucho más fácil" ("[I]t was easier to understand") (Participant 15)." (Colina, 2022, p. 34)

> "Este siento que lo escribió alguien que habla español, alguien que está confortable con el idioma" ("I feel like this one was written by someone who speaks Spanish, someone who's comfortable with the language") (Participant 20)" (Colina, 2022, p. 34)

> "Un cuestionario en la forma A se siente más como un ataque o es tan directo que una persona que habla español se va a sentir que es un rechazo" ["This questionnaire on form A feels more like an attack; it is so direct that a Spanish-speaker is going to feel that it is a rejection"] (Participant 14)" (Colina, 2022, p. 34)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the themes and specific comments participants voiced about each translation's readability, naturalness, and textual features.

> "We identified three themes from the interview responses: overall difficulty of the text, textual features and comprehension, and audience and educational background." (Colina, 2022, p. 34)

### How?

Semistructured interview comments were coded by preference (survey A or B), then reviewed and thematically organized to surface the most frequently repeated themes.

> "For the qualitative analysis, all comments in the semistructured interviews were coded according to preference (for survey A or B). After coding was complete, comments were reviewed and thematically organized. The most often repeated themes were identified." (Colina, 2022, p. 33)

### Who?

The 20 English-Spanish bilingual adults (most Spanish-dominant) reviewing the two Mexican-Spanish translations of the Perceived Stress Scale.

> "We recruited 20 bilingual participants at the campus of the University of Arizona and in its surrounding community using flyers written in English and Spanish and by word of mouth." (Colina, 2022, p. 32)

## Other Notes

This is a qualitative finding grounded in participant voice; the criticisms of translation A (awkward syntax, source-like structure) mirror the higher issue count recorded quantitatively.
