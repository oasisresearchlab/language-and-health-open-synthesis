## Description

![[Idossa_2019_Access_Linguistically-table2.png]]

In a national survey of U.S. blood and marrow transplant (BMT) center staff, more than 90% of respondents reported that Spanish was the most frequently requested non-English language, and this held across all four U.S. census regions (Northeast, Midwest, South, West) (Table 2). In the pooled sample Spanish was named by 75 of 82 respondents (91.5%), and by 100% of respondents in the Northeast, South, and West and 90.9% in the Midwest; the regional difference was not significant (P = 0.2454).

> "More than 90% of respondents indicated that Spanish was the most frequently requested non-English language across all geographic regions, followed by Chinese (29.3%), Arabic (23.2%), Vietnamese (11.0%), and Portuguese (8.5%) (Table 2)." (Idossa, 2019, p. 1034)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: which non-English languages transplant-center patients most often requested, elicited as a survey item on the most requested language.

> "Most often requested languages (in general)" (Idossa, 2019, p. 1034, Table 2)

### How?

A national cross-sectional, web-based survey of transplant-center staff; language-request frequency was captured on Likert scales and, for regional comparison, collapsed into "often"/"not often" and tested with Chi-square or Fisher's exact tests.

> "A national cross-sectional, web-based survey was administered by email to healthcare professionals and staff identified as the primary contact at NMDP/Be The Match network TCs in the U.S. (N = 139) through email." (Idossa, 2019, p. 1032)

### Who?

Staff (predominantly social workers) at NMDP/Be The Match network transplant centers across all U.S. census regions; the regional language table was based on the 77 respondents with known region.

> "Regional variation in requests and difficulty in meeting needs by language (N = 77)" (Idossa, 2019, p. 1034, Table 2)

## Other Notes

The abstract likewise names Spanish first among the languages needing translated materials: "Findings show a significant need for translated patient education materials, especially in Spanish, Chinese, and Arabic" (Idossa, 2019, p. 1031).
