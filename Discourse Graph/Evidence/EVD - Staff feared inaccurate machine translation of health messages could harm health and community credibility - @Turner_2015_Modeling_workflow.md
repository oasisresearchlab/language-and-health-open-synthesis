## Description


![[Turner_2015_Modeling_workflow-table2.png]]
Several health-department staff expressed concern that inaccurate machine translation of health messages could negatively impact health. Their reservations about adopting MT clustered around five worries: that MT gives literal translations and fails to reflect the nuances of language; concern about the accuracy and fluency of output; fear of damaging relationships with the community through poor-quality translations; concern that MT would not meet policy or program requirements; and a belief that post-translation review of MT might be more work than translating from scratch.

> "Several health department staff expressed concern that inaccurate translations of health messages could negatively impact health." (Turner, 2015, p. 142)

> "Concerns expressed about the use of MT for translation included: (1) worry that MT may give literal translations and fails to reflect the nuances of language" (Turner, 2015, p. 142)

> "Anyway that's what makes everyone nervous about machine translation because we know we'll be raked over the coals every time we get it wrong and we just worry with a machine doing it, the chances of there being an error would be high" (Turner, 2015, p. 143, Table 2)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: staff-expressed concerns and reservations about using MT for public-health translation, coded as sub-themes of the attitudes-toward-automation theme.

> "Several health department staff expressed concern that inaccurate translations of health messages could negatively impact health." (Turner, 2015, p. 142)

### How?

Concerns were elicited in the semi-structured interviews and enumerated during thematic coding; representative quotations appear in the themes table (Table 2, sub-theme 9.2).

> "Two researchers analyzed the field notes and transcripts using Braun and Clarke's method of thematic analysis [47] to identify broad categories and the initial coding scheme." (Turner, 2015, p. 138)

### Who?

Health-department staff among the 34 interviewees at PHSKC and WA DOH who develop and translate health-promotion materials.

> "We recruited staff from work units within the departments that frequently performed translations." (Turner, 2015, p. 138)

## Other Notes

This risk-perception finding is distinct from the general "MT quality is poor" appraisal: it specifies why practitioners hesitate to adopt MT (harm to health messaging and community trust), motivating the design requirement that MT errors be tracked and that human review precede public release.
