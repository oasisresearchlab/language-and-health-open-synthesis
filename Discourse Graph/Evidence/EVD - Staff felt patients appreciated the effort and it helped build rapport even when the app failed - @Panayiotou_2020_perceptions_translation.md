## Description

Healthcare staff reported that even when a translation app did not work, patients appreciated the effort of using it and it helped build rapport; there were no reported adverse impacts on the patient relationship from a failed attempt.

> "However, healthcare staff indicated that despite not always working, they felt that the effort was appreciated by their patients and that it often helped to build rapport." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3521)

> "Yet, despite experiences where the app did not work, there were no reported adverse impacts on rapport." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3523)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: staff-perceived effect of attempting to use a translation app on patient rapport, including when the technology failed.

> "What role do you see technology playing in communicating everyday healthcare conversations between older people from CALD backgrounds and their healthcare workers?" (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3518)

### How?

Focus group discussion of staff experiences with translation technology, analysed by inductive content analysis.

> "Two research members (A.P and K.H) who attended the consultations analysed each response to determine meaning and generate codes." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3519)

### Who?

Nursing and allied health staff (n = 17) working in sub-acute/aged care settings across three Melbourne metropolitan public health services.

> "Seventeen healthcare staff from three Melbourne metropolitan public health services took part in the consultations." (Panayiotou, 2020, p. 3519)

## Other Notes

Qualitative relational finding; no associated figure/table (text-only). A benefit that persists even under app failure, distinct from successful translation.
