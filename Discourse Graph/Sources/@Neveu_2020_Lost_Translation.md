abstract: 'During the Coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic, in-person interpreters have
  been deemed "nonessential," and thus eliminated to minimize viral exposure and conserve
  personal protective equipment. Considering alarming patterns of interpreter underuse,
  we evaluate how substitution for remote modalities (telephone or video) may exacerbate
  existing inequalities for patients with limited English proficiency. The inherent
  intimacy, dynamic physicality, and cultural nuances of labor and delivery pose unique
  communication challenges. Using clinical scenarios, we illustrate the vital role
  interpreters have in providing accessible obstetric care. We argue that eliminating
  in-person interpreters in this setting is not justified by COVID-related harms given
  the potential to exacerbate underlying health disparities.
