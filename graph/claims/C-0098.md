# C-0098 — Limited English proficiency is associated with reduced access to surgical care

## Narrative synthesis

Across the review's 6 access-to-surgical-care studies, LEP was significantly associated with reduced access to care in adjusted analysis in 4 of 6. The review notes heterogeneity: 1 of those studies found the association only among non–Spanish-speaking LEP patients, and 2 studies performed only unadjusted analyses and found no association. Because all included studies were observational with residual confounding, the review reports directionality of evidence rather than a pooled estimate; the certainty/GRADE appraisal is left for expert review.

> "Six included studies assessed access to surgical care... Four of these 6 studies found LEP to be significantly associated with reduced access to care in adjusted analysis. However, 1 of these studies revealed a significant association only in patients with non–Spanish-speaking LEP. Two studies performed only unadjusted analyses and identified no association between LEP and limited access to care." (Joo, 2023, p. 7)

> "Surgical patients with limited English proficiency were more likely to experience reduced access in 4 of 6 studies, delays in obtaining care in 3 of 4 studies, longer surgical admission length of stay in 6 of 14 studies, and more likely discharge to a skilled facility than patients with English proficiency in 3 of 4 studies." (Joo, 2023, p. 1)
