# C-0188 — Using English-speaking family members as ad hoc interpreters is a double-edged practice for LEP patient care

Relying on English-speaking family members as ad hoc interpreters is a double-edged practice for LEP patient care: it can facilitate faster, more invested communication when certified interpreters are hard to obtain, yet providers avoid it for surgical consent and legally complex matters and note it can introduce error, extra work, and confusion.
