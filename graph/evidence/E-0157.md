# E-0157 — Documented consent concordance was lower for less commonly spoken languages (Spanish 89.2% vs Tagalog 47.6%)

## Description

![](/attachments/Karen_2024_Impact_Using-table1.png)

Under eConsent, the rate of documented language-concordant interpreter-mediated consent varied by the patient's preferred language, and patients speaking less widely spoken languages had lower concordance (Table 1). The most common languages had the highest concordance (Spanish 89.2%, Chinese–Mandarin 89.1%, Russian 87.0%), while several less common languages were much lower (Farsi 71.4%, Punjabi 59.1%, Tagalog 47.6%). Tagalog was the only listed language with majority-discordant consents (52.4% discordant).

> "Patients with a preferred language that was less widely spoken had lower rates of documented language-concordant interpreter-mediated consents, but the rates were still higher than before eConsent." (Trang, 2024, p. 529)

> "Spanish* 89.2 (421) 10.8 (51) ... Tagalog 47.6 (10) 52.4 (11)" (Trang, 2024, p. 530, Table 1)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the proportion of eConsent encounters with documented language-concordant interpreter-mediated consent, stratified by patient preferred language.

> "Patients with Limited English Proficiency Who Have Documentation of Language-Concordant Interpreter-Mediated Consent vs. Those Who Were Consented in a Language Discordant from Their Documented Preferred Language" (Trang, 2024, p. 530, Table 1)

### How?

Cross-sectional tabulation of the 1,016 study-period eConsent encounters by recorded preferred language, with each language's concordant/discordant counts and percentages reported (Table 1) for the A-0007.

> "Chi-square tests were used to compare categorical variables, and all analysis was performed using R statistical software (version 4.3.0, R Core Team, 2023)." (Trang, 2024, p. 529)

### Who?

The 1,016 LEP eConsent encounters spanning 49 non-English preferred languages at an urban academic medical center.

> "A total of 1,016 eConsent encounters for patients with LEP were included, with patients speaking 49 different languages, most commonly Spanish (46.5%), Chinese (22.1%), and Russian (6.8%)." (Trang, 2024, p. 528)

## Other Notes

The authors speculate lower concordance for less common languages may reflect greater difficulty obtaining interpreter services, greater willingness to use a family member, or patients' limited English use in the consent process (Trang, 2024, p. 530). Languages with an available written consent template are asterisked in Table 1 (Spanish, Chinese–Mandarin, Russian, Arabic, Chinese–Cantonese).

## Caveats

- **Single-institution retrospective interim analysis with a short study period and few supported languages** The findings come from a single urban academic medical center, are retrospective, cover only a short study period (an interim analysis, Feb 8 – Jun 14 2023), and involve a limited number of languages with translated written consent templates (five). These features limit generalizability of the post-implementation concordance rates and the language/service/urgency subgroup patterns to other settings, later time points, and other language groups.
