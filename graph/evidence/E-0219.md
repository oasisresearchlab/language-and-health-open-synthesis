# E-0219 — Chronic opioid use was more frequent among patients needing translation services (p=0.001)

## Description

![](/attachments/Kapoor_2023_Impact_Need-table1.png)

In the full unmatched cohort, the incidence of chronic opioid use was slightly but significantly higher among patients needing translation services than among English-proficient patients (p = 0.001) (Table 1). This is a baseline characteristic of the population needing language assistance.

> "Interestingly, the incidence of chronic opioid use was also slightly but significantly higher in patients needing (n = 100. 8.1%) translation services than those who were English proficient (n = 200.,5.6%, p = 0.001)." (Kapoor, 2023, p. 4)

**Source note (flagged):** the cell counts/percentages as printed ("n = 100. 8.1%" for needing vs "n = 200.,5.6%" for English proficient) are confusingly presented and appear cross-tabulated against Table 1's chronic-opioid-use rows rather than a clean group comparison. The quote is copied verbatim (including the original typographic errors). The finding is captured at the authors' asserted level: a significant association (p = 0.001), chronic opioid use more frequent among LAS-needing patients.

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: prevalence of a history of chronic opioid use, compared by need for translation services.

> "comorbidities (i.e., ... history of chronic pain and opioid use)... were obtained from electronic medical records (EMR)." (Kapoor, 2023, p. 2)

### How?

Retrospective EMR review; categorical association tested with chi-square / Fisher's exact test on the unmatched cohort.

> "The chi-square test or Fisher's exact test was used to evaluate the association between two categorical variables." (Kapoor, 2023, p. 2)

### Who?

The full unmatched cohort of 4791 adults undergoing open abdominal oncologic surgery at a single academic cancer center, 2016–2021.

> "A total of 4791 patients were included from our database (Table 1)." (Kapoor, 2023, p. 3)

## Other Notes

Preoperative opioid use was one of the covariates included in the propensity-score model, so this baseline imbalance was adjusted for in the matched analysis.
