# E-0271 — Patients with a primary language other than English or Spanish did not have higher odds (OR 0.9) of signing consent for studies with Spanish consent at opening

## Description

![](/attachments/Maria_2023_Consent_document-table4.png)

A null that reveals the effect is language-specific: patients whose primary language was other than English *or* Spanish did **not** have higher odds of signing consent for studies that had a Spanish consent document available at study opening — OR 0.9 (95% CI 0.6–1.3), interval spanning 1 (Extended Data Table 4). Availability of a translated document only raised enrollment for speakers of the language that was actually translated (Spanish), not for non-English speakers in general.

> "Patients with a primary language other than English or Spanish did not have higher odds of signing consent documents for studies that had Spanish consent at study opening (OR 0.9, 95% CI 0.6 to 1.3)." (Maria, 2023, p. 858)

## Methods Context

### What?

The observable: the odds that a patient whose primary language was other than English or Spanish signed consent, comparing studies with vs without a Spanish consent document at study opening.

> "Odds for patients with a primary language other than English or Spanish of signing consent to studies with a Spanish translation compared to studies without them." (Maria, 2023, Extended Data Table 4)

### How?

GEE logistic regression clustered by patient, same study-opening subset as the Spanish-speaker analysis but for the non-Spanish, non-English subgroup.

> "Odds ratios (with 95% CI) were estimated from a logistic regression model with Generalized Estimating Equations clustered by patient unique identifier." (Maria, 2023, Extended Data Table 4)

### Who?

Patients with a primary language other than English or Spanish across studies at one cancer centre, 2013–2018.

> "At study opening" (Maria, 2023, Extended Data Table 4)

## Other Notes

This is a genuine null (CI includes 1); it should oppose any claim that translated-consent availability raises enrollment for non-English speakers *regardless of which language is translated*, and it bounds the OR-5.7 Spanish result to language-matched availability.
